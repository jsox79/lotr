# REGIONAL POSITIONS & BATTLE — CANON

_Status: CURRENT CANON_
_Date: 2026-09-14_

## Design Intent

Combat must be consequential without routinely deleting a player's developed stack in a single turn. Battles primarily change position, control, readiness and long-term strength through persistent attrition. Total annihilation should be exceptional rather than the normal result of one engagement.

**A battle should usually leave both players with a story, not leave one player without a game.**

---

## Greater Region Positions

Every Greater Region contains **at least three playable internal Positions**.

- Positions are tactical spaces inside a Greater Region.
- A region may contain more than three Positions when its geography/gameplay warrants it.
- Position topology is region-specific. Regions are not required to use identical three-node lines.
- Positions should be represented as meaningful geography, settlements, routes, fortifications, landmarks or terrain within the articulated region artwork.
- Named Lesser Locations and major landmarks may occupy or modify Positions.
- Examples include Helm's Deep in Rohan, Minas Tirith in Gondor, Mount Doom in Mordor and Orthanc/Isengard within Isengard.

### Internal Movement

Moving between Positions inside the same Greater Region is **not crossing a regional boundary** and therefore does **not** draw that region's Movement card.

Regional Movement Decks remain tied to entering or passing through Greater Regions, not ordinary tactical repositioning inside one.

### Position Control vs Regional Control

Position control and Greater Region control are distinct.

- Opposing forces may occupy/control different Positions inside the same Greater Region.
- A region may therefore be **Contested** without requiring all forces to occupy one abstract space.
- Winning one Position does not automatically mean conquering the entire Greater Region.
- Exact Regional Control requirements may depend on key/control Positions and the broader Region Claiming rules.

This structure allows campaigns to unfold through a region instead of one battle automatically flipping an entire country.

---

## Retreat

Retreat normally occurs **within the Greater Region first** when an eligible internal Position is available.

A defeated force may fall back from one Position to another legal Position rather than immediately leaving the Greater Region.

If no legal internal retreat exists, the force may be forced out of the Greater Region through a legal regional connection.

A normal forced retreat does **not** draw a Regional Movement card. Specific cards, abilities or effects may create a **Harried Retreat** or equivalent that explicitly causes a Movement-card resolution.

Retreat preserves surviving forces while conceding tactical ground.

# Battle Resolution

## 1. Establish Participating Forces

Determine the participating Heroes, Bannermen/Supporting Heroes, Allies, Enemies and applicable Artifacts, Titles, Boons, faction effects, Position effects and temporary modifiers.

## 2. Calculate Total Strength

Each side calculates its total participating Strength after modifiers.

## 3. Determine Winner and Victory Margin

Compare total Strength.

The difference is the **Victory Margin**.

| Victory Margin | Battle Result | Base Damage to Losing Force |
|---:|---|---:|
| 0 | Standoff | 0 |
| 1–2 | Narrow Victory | 1 |
| 3–5 | Strong Victory | 2 |
| 6–8 | Crushing Victory | 3 |
| 9+ | Rout | 4 |

Strength determines battle dominance; it is **not dealt directly as damage**.

This prevents large developed stacks from automatically erasing one another merely because their total Strength values are high.

## 4. Assign Damage

The losing force assigns the battle's Base Damage among eligible participating characters/units, subject to card abilities and effects.

Damage reduces HP and **persists after battle until healed or otherwise removed**.

Persistent wounds create campaign attrition: a force may survive a battle but become progressively less capable of sustaining future engagements.

Existing character, Ally and Enemy abilities that prevent, redirect, increase or react to Damage continue to apply according to their wording.

## 5. Losing Force — Retreat or Stand

After normal battle damage and reactions resolve, the losing player normally chooses:

### Retreat

Move the surviving force to a legal retreat Position.

The victor takes/holds the battlefield Position and may affect Position or Regional Control according to the control rules.

### Stand

The losing force refuses to yield the Position.

- Take **+1 additional Damage**.
- The force becomes **Battle-Worn** until its next Refresh.
- The Position remains contested/occupied as appropriate rather than being freely surrendered.

Standing represents deliberately accepting greater attrition to hold strategically important ground.

Specific effects may prohibit retreat, force retreat, alter Stand, or destroy a defeated unit instead. Existing explicit card text overrides the general rule.

## 6. Standoff

On equal total Strength, neither side wins the Position by default and neither side takes Base Damage from the margin table.

Card abilities/reactions may still resolve. The Position remains contested unless an effect says otherwise.

---

## Battle-Worn

A force that has resolved a player-initiated Battle is considered **Battle-Worn** for the remainder of that turn unless an effect explicitly says otherwise.

A force may normally participate in only **one player-initiated Battle per turn**.

Battle-Worn prevents a single optimized stack from sweeping through several regions/Positions and destroying multiple developed forces in one turn. Abilities may explicitly override this restriction.

Movement or repositioning after battle may still occur when rules/abilities permit; Battle-Worn specifically restricts additional ordinary player-initiated Battles.

---

## Winner's Battle Advantage

After winning a Battle, the victor chooses one Battle Advantage when applicable:

- **Press the Attack** — deal +1 Damage to the losing force before its retreat/stand resolution.
- **Take the Field** — prioritize securing the Position and receive the applicable Position/control advantage.
- **Let Them Flee** — allow the retreat without pressing casualties, favoring readiness/positioning and avoiding unnecessary pursuit.

Exact secondary readiness/control bonuses for Take the Field and Let Them Flee may be tuned during balance testing, but the canonical principle is that victory presents a strategic choice between **greater attrition, stronger territorial gain, or conserving momentum** rather than automatically maximizing casualties.

---

## Annihilation

Ordinary battle is not designed to erase a healthy developed stack in one resolution.

Complete destruction remains possible through:

- accumulated wounds from multiple engagements;
- an overwhelming Rout against already damaged forces;
- inability to retreat combined with continued pressure;
- explicit Enemy/Hero/Event/Artifact abilities;
- legendary threats such as the Balrog;
- deliberate high-risk decisions to Stand;
- other effects that explicitly destroy rather than merely damage.

Thus annihilation is an event with narrative and strategic weight rather than the default combat outcome.

---

## Relationship to Existing Combat Canon

This procedure expands the existing Conflict structure:

**Compare total Strength → Assign Damage → Resolve reactions/abilities → Resolve retreats/destructions.**

Existing card-specific combat text remains authoritative where it creates an exception to these general rules.

---

## TV / Region View

Internal Positions are a major purpose of Region View.

At World View, the TV may summarize forces within a Greater Region. When combat, movement or an important regional action occurs, the camera may zoom into the articulated region and show the actual Positions occupied by Hero/company markers, Enemies and other public forces.

Retreat should therefore be visually understandable: the defeated company physically falls back across the region rather than simply disappearing from one region label and appearing elsewhere.

---

## North Stars

**Combat changes ownership more often than it destroys armies.**

**Wounds make future battles dangerous. Position makes victory meaningful. Retreat keeps the war alive.**
